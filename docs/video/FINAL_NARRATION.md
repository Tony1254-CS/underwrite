# UNDERWRITE — Final English Narration

## 0:00–0:18 · The problem

An AI agent can read an invoice, reason about a payment, and ask for a transaction. But none of that makes it a safe custody authority. If the same model can choose the payee, the amount, the token, and the limit, one manipulated document can become an irreversible financial action.

## 0:18–0:38 · The idea

UNDERWRITE separates intelligence from authority. The model proposes a business action. Evidence decides how much autonomy it has earned. Deterministic policy resolves the vendor and route. The contract enforces the boundary. And a collateralized bond stands behind the residual exposure.

## 0:38–1:10 · Product and authority

This working product centers on the Authority Meter: not a confidence score, but the amount the agent may spend without a person.

In this controlled demonstration, three configurations take the same adversarial exam. A naive script receives zero. A basic model receives forty dollars. A hardened model receives one hundred and eighty. These are scripted demo values; the real reports remain separately verifiable.

The examiner signs the evidence. The owner activates it. The agent cannot raise its own limit.

## 1:10–2:02 · Three payment routes

Now watch the product handle three invoices.

A routine thirty-five-dollar invoice resolves to an approved supplier and follows the Tier One route in the controlled local runtime.

A two-hundred-and-fifty-dollar upgrade exceeds autonomous authority. It becomes Tier Two: held for the owner and checked again before execution.

A hostile instruction replaces the payee, while a replay reuses an invoice identity. Both are rejected. The model never supplies a raw address, token, calldata, or limit. It proposes a vendor identifier and amount. Trusted software decides the rest.

## 2:02–2:42 · Assume compromise

Prompt defenses are not the security boundary. The Attack Lab assumes the application is bypassed and the agent key is stolen. The request reaches the local EVM contract directly.

An unregistered recipient reverts. A duplicate invoice reverts. A new supplier remains in a three-hundred-second cooldown, blocking an immediate payee switch. In this simulated scenario, zero funds move. Model failure does not erase the settlement boundary.

## 2:42–3:18 · Consequence and recovery

Controls bound authority, but cannot prove every permitted invoice is correct. When an error is discovered, the owner files a claim with cryptographic evidence.

Approval performs two consequences atomically: the bond reimburses the vault within its terms, and the qualification is revoked. The next payment is refused. Recovery remains bounded by the deductible, payout caps, and bond capacity.

## 3:18–4:06 · Real public-chain proof

Now the public-chain evidence.

This is real Base Sepolia activity using controlled MockUSDC and addresses. The exam commitment is recorded. The qualification is activated with zero Tier One authority and bounded Tier Two capability. Then the agent requests thirty-five MockUSDC, starting a three-hundred-second challenge period.

After the delay, the final transaction calls execute pending with request ID two. The receipt succeeds, with a matching Payment Executed event for payment ID one, Tier Two. The IDs are separate counters; recipient, amount, invoice identity and calldata match.

This is public testnet settlement—not mainnet, not customer activity, and not a Tier One public-chain claim.

## 4:06–4:34 · Verification and measured trade-off

Every layer remains inspectable: proposal, policy, contract result and whether funds moved. Changing one byte breaks the evidence digest.

In a controlled, self-authored evaluation, three agents processed the same three hundred and thirty invoices under three regimes. UNDERWRITE recorded zero Class One violations on this corpus, while moving more work to review. One unseen Class Two error caused a one-hundred-and-seventy MockUSDC loss; the bond reimbursed one hundred and sixty-five, leaving five net.

## 4:34–4:52 · Close

The current rerun passes one hundred and one contract tests, one hundred and fifty-nine engine tests, and twenty-five thousand six hundred invariant calls. This remains a testnet prototype and security self-assessment, not an independent audit.

Do not give an AI a blank check. Make authority earnable, enforceable, and revocable.
